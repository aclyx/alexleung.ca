## 2026-10-29 stating limits and house style

A site audit found grader vocabulary in published posts (“I did not establish…”, “does not establish that…”). The voice gained a rule to state limits as what was done or observed, six calibration passages, and a house style section; the grader's local clarity pass gained a matching check. All reviews used fresh contexts with inherited model settings and no overrides. Reviewers could not save `report.md` files, so each returned its report as text and the coordinating session summarized it.

First wording, frozen before the independent flapper pair was created:

- P caught the generic testing caveat, now also described as reviewer phrasing, and the missing cost valuation. It also flagged the unsourced “often.”
- K needed no editorial repairs. Its reviewer kept “Four walks are not enough…” as reader-facing; the three-minute buffer remained an optional note.
- R drew its first required editorial repair in any run: cut “two punctual returns still tell me little about what would happen with a delay.” That sentence is the bounded uncertainty the case preserves, so this was an over-trigger.
- S2 caught its verification-style sentences and kept the shared unknown, but offered deleting limits as one fix; the key prefers restating them.
- S1 kept the shared unknown and two plain limits but asked to cut “I only worked on the upstairs toilet and left the fill valve alone,” partly as reviewer-facing, contrary to the key.

The wording was then narrowed using R alone and frozen before the flapper results were read: the concern is phrasing about what checks did not establish, and an unknown that bears on the reader's judgment stays.

Second wording:

- R needed no editorial repairs; its reviewer kept the delay sentence as a plainly stated risk.
- P repeated its three findings.
- S2 caught the target and restated the water-meter limit plainly, while still offering deletion for two limits.
- S1 asked to cut two plain limits and called the water-meter sentence (“I never read the water meter, so I can't say…”) reviewer-facing. The key allows cutting that limit from both drafts as an editorial choice, but not that label.

Reviewers also applied the new house style to fixtures, flagging straight apostrophes such as “o'clock.” Fixtures were not edited; treat that as expected mechanical noise. All flapper reviews flagged a dropped “visible” qualifier shared by both drafts; it is a fixture artifact, not the tested distinction.

The narrowed wording fixed the R over-trigger without losing P or S2. It did not stop reviewers from cutting low-stakes limits that are already stated plainly; both S1 runs did so. The flapper pair did not inform tuning and was added as a regression case. These are single-run spot checks, not reliability measurements.
