# Blog Post Grader

Use this rubric to grade alexleung.ca blog drafts before publishing. It operationalizes the canonical [writing voice](../../../../docs/writing-voice.md); the goal is fit with Alex's voice, not generic polish.

## Scoring

Score out of 100 after completing the evidence-based checks. A publish-ready draft should score **90+** with no blocking issue and all applicable pass/fail checks satisfied. A high total cannot compensate for missing coherence or an unearned ending. Treat scores as editorial judgments, not calibrated measurements; identify the smallest revision set that addresses any failed check.

- 90-100: Strong fit. Only minor edits remain.
- 80-89: Usable direction, but the post needs targeted revision before publishing.
- 70-79: Clear idea, but voice, structure, evidence, or scope is not yet reliable.
- Below 70: Rework from the user's facts and thesis before doing sentence-level edits.

## Rubric

### 1. Thesis and Value: 15 points

- Names a real tension, mechanism, or observation rather than a vague topic.
- Makes the reader's takeaway legible without turning the post into a pitch.
- Keeps the claim sized to the user's actual experience and evidence.
- If the user supplied a main takeaway, preserves that mechanism instead of substituting an adjacent generic lesson.
- Preserves the supplied mechanism, intended scope, and explicit future aspiration. Concrete examples demonstrate the broader idea without replacing it.
- Preserves the takeaway semantically without restating it after every section or across every editorial surface.

### 2. Concrete Grounding: 15 points

- Opens from a concrete behavior, object, constraint, observed change, or encounter with an idea when the post is reflective. A philosophical reflection can be grounded in reading and a specific intellectual reaction without a physical scene.
- Orients the reader in the first sentence by naming the subject, workflow, object, or setting before moving into comparison, measurement, or interpretation.
- Uses specific examples, facts, mechanisms, or comparisons instead of abstract framing. Defines central unfamiliar concepts enough to follow the reasoning; categories and citations alone do not establish that a reader understands them.
- Distinguishes logistical specificity from lived material. Place names, times, distances, and route sequence establish orientation but do not by themselves count as a lived observation.
- Pays off salient setups such as new gear, excess supplies, or difficult travel with a user-supplied result, evaluation, trade-off, consequence, or preference when the post depends on them.
- Lets concrete details stand when they already carry the point; any following interpretation adds a distinct consequence, decision, or changed understanding.
- Research-backed claims are dated when needed and linked to credible sources.

### 3. Alex Voice Fit: 20 points

- Uses first person when grounding Alex's own experience or judgment and stays measured and direct elsewhere.
- Feels technically grounded and clearly experienced through clarity, evidence, mechanisms, and trade-offs rather than self-description.
- Makes supported claims with quiet confidence instead of drifting into promotion, vagueness, or unnecessary hedging.
- Balances rigor with warmth, curiosity, approachability, and a human perspective rather than becoming sterile. Preserves supported uncertainty and dry observations; first-person phrasing alone does not make a passage personal.
- Avoids hype, influencer packaging, recruiter language, slogans, and broad future-of-work claims.
- Avoids polished thesis scaffolding, rhetorical contrast templates, decorative metaphors, and phrasing that sounds like it is performing sophistication.
- Avoids draft-wide density of balanced contrasts, tidy inventories, generic evaluations, and recap sentences that makes the voice feel generated.

### 4. Structure and Flow: 15 points

- Each main section advances one clear claim. In arguments and philosophical reflections, the prose shows how that claim advances or complicates the central question; shared vocabulary or inclusion of the requested topics is insufficient.
- The form fits the genre instead of defaulting to an orientation, 2-4 headings, and a closing moral.
- Paragraph cadence is varied and mostly multi-sentence.
- Connections work between adjacent sentences as well as sections. Use the local clarity and coherence evidence procedures below to separate what the prose states from what the reviewer supplies. Brief personal asides are allowed.
- The ending fits the genre: it may return to an argument's tension, stop on a personal or travel scene, or leave a case study with a concrete limit or next experiment. In an argument or philosophical reflection, it accounts for material complications raised in the body without mechanically recapping sections or claiming an unsupported resolution.

### 5. Truthfulness and Scope Control: 15 points

- Preserves user-provided facts exactly.
- Matches the source-fact ledger in actor, value, scope, precision, and causal, temporal, or comparative relationships.
- Does not invent anecdotes, metrics, usage duration, ownership, or confidence.
- Marks uncertainty honestly instead of converting it into a polished conclusion.
- Keeps future aspirations clearly bounded as goals rather than presenting them as current capabilities or guarantees.
- Does not add links to later-dated posts without intentionally updating chronology metadata.

### 6. Blog Surface Fit: 10 points

- Title, excerpt, tags, headings, cover alt text, and metadata feel like the same editorial system.
- Title and excerpt orient a reader without access to the body. Check the actual title-plus-excerpt/card or metadata context first; the body cannot repair a reference missing from that surface.
- Title is concise, spoken, and subject- or mechanism-specific, not a category label, clickbait frame, or clever essay title.
- Headings are plain and literal unless the post has already earned a more essayistic frame.
- Tags are precise and non-overlapping.

### 7. Visual and Asset Fit: 10 points

- Cover or inline imagery reveals the actual subject, object, state, or thesis.
- Generated images are grounded and content-revealing, not decorative filler.
- Images with Alex refer to Alex by name in alt/caption-style metadata.
- Inline images are only present when they clarify the argument or make a model/example inspectable.
- Every served raster blog source and generated variant uses a `.webp` extension and contains genuine RIFF/WEBP bytes. Playwright PNG baselines are test artifacts and are outside this check.

## Review Order and Local Clarity

1. Read a **surface-only snapshot** of the title, excerpt, and any distinct card/metadata copy before opening the body, ledger, or comparison posts. Save a short cold-read note: what subject and promise are clear, and which references lack context on that surface. A title may supply context for its excerpt; the unseen body may not. Do not require every short field to restate the title or define every named concept. Unfamiliar terminology alone is not a broken reference: when the topic and question are legible, further explanation can be optional. Inspect actual rendered copy when available, and distinguish that from a source-only review.
2. Read the body alone. For arguments and philosophical reflections, record the coherence evidence below. For every genre, make one local clarity pass before consulting the ledger:
   - **Sentence connections:** check adjacent claims and definitions, not only section transitions. Name the missing relationship when a reader must supply it. Consider moving a definition or paragraph before adding connective prose; a shared topic is not always enough.
   - **References:** resolve phrases such as “that future,” “the same question,” and “this” against the nearby prose. Name the referent when it is ambiguous or too remote. Keep ordinary demonstratives whose referent is clear; this is not a word ban.
   - **Repeated meaning and empty explanation:** flag repeated reactions or conclusions and explanations that only label something interesting, difficult, or beyond us. Keep a reaction that adds personal meaning. Where reasoning needs a mechanism or consequence, request the smallest supported detail; never invent one. Preserve necessary applications of a concept even when they repeat a noun.
3. Save the draft-only findings, then read the ledger and corpus. Check intent, fact delta, and patterns without using prior approval to erase a clarity finding.

Report material clarity defects with the quoted sentence pair or reference, its reading cost, and the smallest repair. Keep these findings within structure/flow, voice, and surface fit; do not add a second score or a table for every sentence. Distinguish a required clarity repair from an optional cadence preference. Open questions and brief asides need no forced resolution.

## Coherence Evidence

For arguments and philosophical reflections, complete this check from the draft before reading the ledger. Limit it to concepts and connections that carry the central argument; it is not a template for every paragraph or for travel/photo posts.

Record a compact evidence table for each central concept:

| Step                    | Evidence to quote from the draft                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Definition or mechanism | What the concept means or how it works.                                                          |
| Applied inference       | What follows from that concept in this post’s specific situation, including relevant conditions. |
| Consequence             | How that inference changes the central question, stakes, judgment, or unresolved tension.        |

Mark a step **stated**, **implicit**, or **missing**. Quote the relevant clause; label your own connective reasoning as an inference by the reviewer. A definition, list, or possible example cannot by itself establish the general implication the post relies on. Quoting two endpoints does not show that the draft makes the step between them. In particular, distinguish what could happen in one scenario from what a mechanism gives reason to expect across scenarios. Do not turn a conditional tendency into a guarantee to make a connection sound stronger.

A sentence can perform several steps, and ordinary implications need no ceremonial explanation. Require a revision when a main concept’s relevance rests on the reader supplying its substantive application or consequence, even if an expert can reconstruct a plausible argument. State the smallest missing proposition. Do not require repetitions of an already stated inference, or treat an open philosophical question as a missing answer. After consulting the ledger, perform the intent coverage check below; logical sufficiency alone does not establish fidelity to the requested emphasis.

For the important transitions, name the relationship the prose needs: contrast, addition, return to an earlier question, cause/consequence, or example. Quote the wording and identify the claims or question it connects. If a contrast word implies an opposition the prose has not established, recommend an additive or returning transition. Do not ban particular words: a defensible contrast can pass, and an unresolved personal aside does not need to become a premise. Report an optional preference as optional rather than inventing a logical error.

A fluent reviewer summary or a high score cannot substitute for this evidence. Keep this analysis inside the whole-piece coherence check; do not add a second overlapping score.

## Intent Coverage

Keep the ledger in three distinct parts: **current facts and semantic intent**, **explicit verbatim constraints**, and **earlier approved wording or superseded history**. Preserve source quotations and qualifiers in the relevant part; ordinary approval of a proposed draft does not make its wording mandatory or prove its quality. Use the latest instruction where requests conflict, and omit obsolete history from grader inputs unless needed to interpret a current constraint. Flag a clarity problem in explicitly required wording without silently changing it.

After the draft-only reading, compare the user’s substantive requested points with the prose. For each requested mechanism, relationship, scope, or transition role, quote the request and the passage that fulfills it; label it **explicit**, **partial/implicit**, or **absent**. This belongs in main-takeaway fidelity, not in a new score.

A draft can be logically coherent while leaving out the inference the author wanted to make. Do not substitute a definition, adjacent example, or narrower defensible claim for a requested application or consequence. If the user asks to express a conditional tendency across cases, a single possible case does not fulfill that scope. A partial or absent substantive point needs revision even when the narrower argument would stand on its own. Surface paraphrase is fine; exact wording is not required. Preserve factual qualifications instead of reproducing an unsupported absolute from rough notes.

Use the prose alone to judge logical validity; use the brief or ledger to judge intended emphasis and transition role. Report those judgments separately so a wording preference does not become a fabricated logical defect. Preserve the pre-ledger evidence labels and record any intent gap discovered afterward.

## Blocking Issues

Mark a grading result as blocked even if the numeric score is high when any of these are present:

- Any fact delta against the supplied ledger: an invented or unsupported personal detail, a changed actor, value, scope, or qualifier, or an added causal, temporal, or comparative relationship.
- A personal or travel draft missing either a supported condition, scene, or observation or a supported reaction, evaluation, consequence, trade-off, or preference, unless the user explicitly requested a bare trip log or caption-led photo essay.
- A misleading factual claim, stale current-state claim, or unsupported statistic.
- Tone that reads as hype, self-promotion, influencer packaging, corporate or resume language, sterile formality, or unsupported vagueness.
- A dominant pattern of balanced contrasts, polished lists, generic interpretations after concrete details, or recap sentences that makes the draft read as templated.
- A structure that closely repeats recent posts despite a different genre or subject, especially the same context block, heading count, and closing moral.
- For an argument or philosophical reflection, a missing connection needed to understand how a main section advances the central question, or an ending that ignores a material complication or asserts an unsupported resolution. Brief personal asides and deliberately unresolved questions are not failures by themselves.
- A reflective opening that summarizes meaning before showing the concrete experience.
- A reflective opening that begins with a comparison, metric, or time jump before naming the actual subject or context for the reader.
- A draft that replaces the user's stated main takeaway with a nearby generic takeaway, such as "know when to stop", "treat it as an operating signal", or "tokens per decision".
- A draft that narrows a stated general philosophy to one concrete example, or omits an explicit future aspiration that materially defines the user's intended story.
- For AI-agent/token-use posts, missing the harness or feedback-loop mechanism when the user explicitly identified it as the point.
- Titles or headings that rely on clever frames, point/lever metaphors, or transformation slogans after the user asked for a plainer style.
- Missing required frontmatter or broken asset references.
- A served raster blog source or generated variant is not genuine WebP, including a PNG or JPEG renamed with a `.webp` extension.
- A new post marked `draft: false` before the current prose passes this full grader, unless the user explicitly waived the editorial gate.

## Fresh-Context Grading Prompt

Run the grader in a fresh subagent context. Pass only the target post, this rubric, a verbatim source-fact ledger that preserves actors, values, scope, precision, and stated relationships, any explicit user-selected bare-log or caption-led-photo-essay mode or editorial-gate waiver, and 3-5 other recent published posts for corpus comparison. Do not pass the author's rationale, known weak spots, previous score, or planned fixes. The comparison posts are evidence for repeated patterns, not style templates.

Provide a surface-only snapshot separately from the body. Follow the review order above: save the isolated surface reading first, then the body-only local clarity and applicable coherence findings, and only then consult the ledger and corpus. Earlier approved phrasing belongs to editorial history, not evidence of clarity.

Use this prompt shape:

```text
Use the attached blog-post grading rubric to grade the draft at <post path>. Work independently from the authoring agent. First read only <surface snapshot> and save surface-only.md. Then read the body and save draft-only.md with local clarity and applicable coherence evidence BEFORE opening the ledger or corpus. Complete intent and fact checks afterward, then assign a score. Return:

1. Score out of 100.
2. Blocking issues, if any.
3. Main-takeaway fidelity: pass/fail, including the intent coverage check. Quote the substantive requested points and their draft evidence, label coverage explicit/partial/absent, and distinguish omissions from logical defects.
4. Opening orientation: pass/fail, with the first sentence quoted or referenced.
5. Surface style: pass/fail, including the saved cold-read finding for title/excerpt and distinct card/metadata copy, plus appropriate headings.
6. Category scores with one-sentence rationale each.
7. The top 3 revision priorities, ordered by expected score impact.
8. Local clarity and voice: report material defects within structure/flow, voice, or surface fit. Quote disconnected sentence pairs, unresolved references, or repeated meaning; give the smallest repair and separate optional preferences. Prior approval is not a reason to dismiss a defect.
9. Pattern density: pass/fail for balanced contrasts, lists, generic interpretations after details, and recaps, citing representative clusters rather than enforcing a word quota.
10. Corpus distinctiveness: pass/fail against the supplied recent posts, naming any repeated opening, section, transition, or ending template.
11. Experiential sufficiency: pass/fail for a personal or travel post; not applicable otherwise. For a personal or travel post, quote both the supported condition, scene, or observation and the supported reaction, evaluation, consequence, trade-off, or preference that make the piece more than an itinerary; logistics alone do not satisfy this check.
12. Fact delta: pass/fail. Identify any claim that does not map to the source-fact ledger with its actor, value, scope, precision, and causal, temporal, or comparative relationships intact.
13. Whole-piece coherence: pass/fail for arguments and philosophical reflections; not applicable otherwise. State the central question and include the coherence evidence table with stated/implicit/missing labels. Check important transition relationships. Identify any substantive step supplied by the reviewer and the smallest required revision, separating that from optional wording preferences. Distinguish brief personal asides from disconnected main sections.
14. Earned ending: pass/fail for arguments and philosophical reflections; not applicable otherwise. Quote how the ending accounts for material complications in the body. A qualified judgment or unresolved question may pass; neither a recap nor a settled answer is required.

Do not rewrite the post. Keep the report concise and evidence-backed with file/line references where possible.
```

## Authoring Loop

1. Build the source-fact ledger using the three-part intent coverage guidance above. Preserve actors, values, scope, precision, stated relationships, and the main takeaway; separate meaning from any explicit verbatim constraint.
2. For personal and travel posts, confirm that the source material contains both required kinds of lived material. Ask one bundled follow-up if either is missing, unless the user requested a bare trip log or caption-led photo essay.
3. Draft or revise the post locally while keeping a new post at `draft: true`.
4. Follow the review order and local clarity pass: isolated surfaces, body-only reasoning and sentence connections, then ledger and corpus.
5. Check pattern density and the ending within that pass. Judge function rather than keyword counts; retain necessary reasoning and avoid forcing unlike genres into one structure.
6. Supply the ledger and 3-5 other recent published posts to a fresh-context grading subagent using the prompt above.
7. If the score is below 90, any applicable pass/fail check fails, or any blocking issue remains, revise from the highest-impact findings first.
8. Re-run the grader in a fresh context after meaningful revisions.
9. Continue until the score is 90+ with no blocking issues and all applicable pass/fail checks succeed.
10. Set `draft: false` only after the current prose passes and the user asks to publish or preview normally, unless the user explicitly waived the editorial gate.
11. If two grading passes fail the editorial gate on the same issue, stop local patching, name the repeated pattern, and ask the user for missing facts or a direction choice instead of smoothing the prose into generic polish.
12. If user feedback exposes a recurring failure and the user asks to improve the harness, update the skill or rubric so future grading catches that failure mode.
