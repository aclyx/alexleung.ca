# Blog Post Grader

Evaluate the draft against the canonical [writing voice](../../../../docs/writing-voice.md) and the user's brief. Judge the text the reader receives, distinguishing necessary repairs from preferences. A fresh perspective is useful only when its findings are supported by the prose.

## Review order and local clarity

1. Read only the title, excerpt, and any distinct card/metadata copy. Save `surface-only.md` before opening the body or ledger. The title can supply context for the excerpt; the unseen body cannot. An unfamiliar term does not automatically need a definition on a short surface. Distinguish rendered inspection from source-only review.
2. Read the body alone. Check the opening, adjacent sentence relationships, references, reasoning, and ending; save `draft-only.md` before consulting the ledger or corpus. Do not use source context to excuse an opening that names an unexplained cleanup or project before establishing its goal. Quote material defects and give the smallest repair. Consider reordering or deletion before adding connective prose.
3. Read the ledger for intent and fact checks, then compare with the supplied recent posts for repeated patterns. Keep the earlier cold-read findings; approved history cannot erase a clarity problem. The corpus reveals repetition, not a template to imitate.

Resolve demonstratives against nearby text. Preserve ordinary references and legitimate contrasts; no word is banned. For suspected filler, read the passage without it: identify the useful distinction, mechanism, evidence, or personal judgment that would be lost. Judge that contribution against what the intended reader knows and what the piece already establishes. A rhetorical role alone does not earn a sentence its place; if it merely sets up another obvious explanation, consider cutting both. Prefer cutting or combining tautological caveats and repeated explanations to polishing them. Keep necessary applications and personal judgments, including a short statement of why a cost is worth paying. Avoid imposing a definition–example–lesson structure on every paragraph.

## Development and paragraph flow

Correct assertions and complete topic coverage do not establish useful depth. After reading the brief, assess whether the piece develops the requested mechanism or judgment enough for its audience. A workflow named in a sentence may still leave the reader unable to understand a consequential choice within it. Identify that missing contribution and the available material that could support it; do not demand length, an anecdote, or an example for its own sake. A short note can be complete, and a long draft can remain superficial.

Read adjacent paragraphs as a continuous passage. Check whether each develops, qualifies, or turns from the preceding thought in a way the reader can follow. Shared subject matter and a plausible outline are not sufficient: several individually sensible tips can still feel disconnected. Quote the affected boundary and explain the missing relationship or distracting detour. Prefer a supported change in sequence, focus, or development over connective filler. Keep this prose-flow judgment separate from logical validity and intent coverage; do not invent a logical gap to describe awkward writing.

## Coherence and ending

For arguments and philosophical reflections, identify the central question. For each concept carrying the reasoning, record a compact table:

| Step                    | Evidence from the draft                                          |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Definition or mechanism | What the concept means or how it works.                          |
| Applied inference       | What follows in this post's situation, including its conditions. |
| Consequence             | How that changes the stakes, judgment, or unresolved question.   |

Mark each step **stated**, **implicit**, or **missing** and quote the relevant clause. Label connective reasoning supplied by the reviewer. Two quoted endpoints do not demonstrate the step between them. A definition, list, or single possible scenario does not establish an implication across scenarios; a conditional tendency is not a guarantee.

Require the smallest missing proposition when the central argument relies on the reader supplying a substantive application or consequence. Ordinary implications need no ceremonial explanation. For important transitions, identify the needed relationship—contrast, addition, return, cause, or example—and assess whether the wording expresses it. Distinguish a logical gap from the author's requested emphasis.

An argumentative ending should account for material complications raised in the body. If it announces a concern, preference, or changed view that the piece depends on, the prose should supply its reason rather than leaving the reviewer to invent one. That reason may appear earlier; do not demand a recap or a formulaic “because” sentence. Brief personal asides and deliberately open questions need no forced resolution. Other genres may end on a scene or observation without making an argument.

## Intent and facts

Keep the ledger in three parts:

- **Current facts and semantic intent:** source quotations preserving actors, values, precision, ownership, uncertainty, scope, and causal, temporal, or comparative relationships. Distinguish available background facts from requested inclusions. Include verified external sources for factual claims drawn from research.
- **Explicit verbatim constraints:** only wording the user has specifically required unchanged.
- **Editorial history:** earlier approved or superseded prose, only when needed to interpret a current constraint. Approval does not make wording mandatory or prove its quality; use the latest instruction when requests conflict.

Available facts are not an inclusion checklist. Tie a required inclusion to the user’s request or to reasoning the piece relies on; do not demand that every background note or ordinary implication be restated. Compare substantive requested points with the draft. Quote the request and corresponding passage; mark coverage **explicit**, **partial/implicit**, or **absent**. Check emphasis as well as presence: examples must support the requested personal idea or mechanism, and a superseded aim must not continue to drive the piece merely because the new aim is mentioned. Preserve the author's valuation of a tradeoff: listing a cost beside a benefit may omit why the author considers it worthwhile, and valued work must not silently become only a burden. Treat substantive omissions as intent gaps even if the prose is logically coherent. Do not invent a logical error to enforce a wording preference, or repeat an unsupported absolute from rough notes when a qualified statement preserves the intended point. Flag unclear explicitly required wording instead of silently changing it.

Check factual claims against their sources. Paraphrase is allowed; changed actors, values, scope, qualifiers, or relationships are fact deltas. Do not infer personal experience, prior expertise, duration, or certainty. Future aspirations remain aspirations. Personal/travel drafts need both sourced observations and sourced reactions, preferences, consequences, or trade-offs, except in an explicitly requested bare-log or caption-led-photo-essay mode. Reading and an intellectual reaction count; physical scenery is not required.

## Decision

Base readiness on supported findings and completed checks. Do not assign a numerical score; it adds false precision to editorial judgment and cannot establish that the author's intent or taste has been met.

Any material intent gap, fact delta, unsupported claim, central clarity/coherence failure, or unearned argumentative ending blocks publication. So do materially underdeveloped reasoning, disconnected prose that obstructs the account, missing required source material, dominant promotional or formulaic prose, and broken frontmatter/assets. Assess these by genre and evidence, not arbitrary word or heading counts. Cite image-workflow evidence for genuine WebP and variant validity; do not infer file bytes from an extension. Apply the dates and chronology rules in [post-template](post-template.md). Mark any unperformed required check as unverified rather than passed.

## Fresh-context inputs and report

For a full-post review, supply separate surface and body snapshots, the full post and asset paths for format verification, this rubric, the canonical voice, and the source-fact ledger. For a scoped revision, supply the affected passage with surrounding context, any changed surfaces, the rubric, voice, and relevant ledger. Include any explicit mode or editorial waiver. Compare a small relevant sample of other published posts for new posts, substantial rewrites, or requested voice/corpus audits; local revisions do not require that comparison.

Inspect frontmatter and assets after the cold reads when they are in scope. Exclude the source conversation, previous reviews, known weak spots, planned repairs, and the drafting agent's defense of its editorial choices. Keep Alex's stated intent, preferences, and reasons in the ledger: those are source material, not review priming. Follow the reading order above for the supplied surfaces.

Return:

- **Decision:** ready, needs revision, or verification incomplete, with the findings or missing checks that determine it.
- **Required repairs:** quoted evidence, its effect on the reader or intent, and the smallest supported fix. Order by impact. Zero repairs is a valid result; do not fill a quota.
- **Check results:** intent priority/coverage, development at the requested depth, paragraph flow, fact delta, orientation/surfaces, local clarity/voice, corpus pattern density, experiential sufficiency, whole-piece coherence/ending, and assets/format. Mark each pass, fail, not applicable, or unverified. Include the intent evidence and applicable coherence table; avoid repeating them under separate headings.
- **Optional preferences or limitations:** only when useful. A clean draft does not need invented improvement suggestions. Do not rewrite the post.

For scoped excerpt or regression reviews, assess only the requested dimensions and report that scope; do not assign a full-post score or publication verdict. Publishing status and the authoring loop are governed by the [main skill](../SKILL.md#editorial-gate).
