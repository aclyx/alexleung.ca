# Editorial Regression Cases

Use this evaluator-only guide when changing coherence, intent coverage, surfaces, local clarity, or ending behavior. Do not give it to ordinary post graders. Fixtures test specific judgments; none is a general publication-readiness benchmark.

## Isolated procedure

1. Freeze the current rubric and canonical voice. Copy the frozen rubric and voice, each relevant case, and its brief into a separate temporary folder with neutral filenames. Keep the other cases, this guide, answer keys, live post, source conversation, previous scores, and planned fixes outside each grader's inputs.
2. For C/D, split title/excerpt into `surface.md` and prose into `body.md`. Give the grader only its folder, the requested dimensions from the table, and an output location. For other cases, supply `body.md`. These are scoped evaluations: no full-post score, source/asset verification, or publication verdict.
3. Start one fresh-context subagent per case. Require `surface-only.md` before body access when a surface is supplied, then `draft-only.md` before brief access. After that, request `report.md` with separate intent and fact judgments, quoted evidence, required repairs, optional preferences, and limitations. Do not let the grader rewrite, browse, inspect unrelated files, or create agents.
4. Compare the reports with the requested scope and expectations only after they are saved. Record the instruction/fixture revisions, available model settings, actual findings, and report locations. Preserve unexpected results; do not feed the intended answer back to a grader and call its retry independent validation.
5. Revise instructions only when the evidence supports it, then rerun affected cases in fresh contexts while retaining earlier reports. Do not edit a fixture to make a failed run pass, demand artificial disagreement, match strings, or treat scores as measurements of reliability.

When generalizing a new rule, also use a different subject not used to tune it. Have an independent agent create a small synthetic pair, neutral brief, and separate key without reading the proposed rubric or worked cases. Freeze the rubric before opening the pair; keep the key away from graders. Once a case has informed tuning, it is a regression fixture, not an unseen test.

## Calibration and limits

The original A/B prose-only reviews passed both cases. Separate intent checking exposed B's missing requested implication without inventing a logical defect. Earlier C/D testing also produced a false alarm about an unfamiliar term in a clear excerpt; that motivated distinguishing an unfamiliar term from a broken reference.

In the 2026-10-04 consolidation run, fresh graders again identified B's two intent gaps and C's unclear surface, missing computing explanation, and underspecified hope. A and D passed their scoped checks. Reviewers did not identify every plausible line edit; successful pairs remain spot checks.

The first ending pair flagged E’s missing requested reason, but also required F to repeat a background fact about creating the intelligence. The intent guidance was clarified to distinguish available source facts from required inclusions. A fresh A–F run retained B’s and C’s findings, required E’s explicit reason, and accepted A, D, and F without required repairs. The earlier reports were retained; the fixtures were not edited to force those results.

The independently created walk pair was first read after the rubric was frozen. Both drafts passed, contrary to the key's intended rejection of K. The reviewer recognized its smaller time buffer but treated spelling out the arithmetic as optional because the preference stayed tentative, the observed times were explicit, and reliability remained uncertain. R made the margin and conditional plan more explicit. The instructions were not tuned to force a rejection. After the background-fact clarification, a fresh run asked K to reconcile its three-minute buffer with its dislike of rushing, while R again needed no repairs. The changed judgment is not evidence that this unrelated rubric edit caused an improvement. Preserve both texts, the original key, and both results: this is a borderline judgment with observed reviewer variation, not demonstrated reliable detection.
