# Before I turn on the stove

I usually start heating the pan while I'm still preparing my after-work tofu, cabbage, and noodles. Getting something onto the heat feels like starting dinner. On the evening I burned the garlic while cutting the cabbage, though, I had started one part of dinner before I could keep up with it.

For the next three versions of the same meal, I left the stove off until I had drained and cut the tofu, sliced the cabbage, and mixed the sauce. I gathered the prepared ingredients on a tray beside the stove. That introduced more bowls to wash, but it also changed what was left to do once the pan was hot: I could watch the food instead of deciding whether I had time to finish chopping.

I haven't timed the two approaches, so I don't know whether I made dinner any faster. The useful difference was that I could finish a preparation job without another part of the meal already needing attention. Mixing the sauce beforehand was still work; it just wasn't work I had to fit around a hot pan.

Adding the noodles at the end still felt rushed. Having the ingredients ready didn't make every cooking step easy, and three dinners aren't enough reason to reorganize my whole kitchen. They were enough to make me want to keep this order for this meal, even with the extra washing up.

Next time, I'll get the tofu, cabbage, and sauce ready before switching on the stove. The tray is a convenient place to put them, but I don't need to turn it into a requirement that every ingredient has its own bowl. I want to reach the pan with the chopping finished and the sauce mixed.
