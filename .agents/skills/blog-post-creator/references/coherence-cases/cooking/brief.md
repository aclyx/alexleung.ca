# Author brief

Write a short personal blog post for people who enjoy ordinary accounts of cooking at home. Readers do not need a recipe or an introduction to kitchen terminology.

The intended point is that doing a little preparation before heating the pan made one familiar weeknight dinner easier to manage. Explore the practical difference with enough detail and connection for the reader to understand why it helped and where it stopped helping. Keep the scale modest: this is a personal observation from three dinners, not a tested efficiency method or advice for every cook.

Supported facts:

- The author regularly makes tofu, cabbage, and noodles after work.
- They usually start heating the pan while they are still preparing the ingredients.
- On one occasion, the garlic burned while they were cutting the cabbage.
- For the next three versions of this dinner, they drained and cut the tofu, sliced the cabbage, and mixed the sauce before turning on the stove.
- They put the prepared ingredients together on a tray beside the stove.
- This meant more bowls to wash.
- Once cooking started, they could stay with the pan instead of leaving it to finish chopping or mix the sauce.
- They did not time the dinners and cannot say whether the process saved time.
- Adding the noodles at the end still felt rushed.
- They intend to keep preparing the tofu, cabbage, and sauce before heating the pan, without making a rule that every ingredient needs its own bowl.

Do not invent timings, exact quantities, other dinners, another person's reaction, or a broader history of using this approach.
