# Evaluator-only key

Keep this file out of evaluator context until the independent assessments have been recorded.

## Intended distinction

- `draft-n.md` is the polished but underdeveloped case.
- `draft-m.md` is the developed and connected case.

The pair shares an author brief, a title, a factual premise, the same three-dinner scope, and the same practical decision. Both are intended to be readable, factually supportable personal drafts. The weaker case is not a spelling, grammar, safety, accuracy, or verbosity trap.

The underdeveloped draft offers a competent sequence of observations but largely leaves them as separate statements. Its concessions arrive as checklist items: extra washing up, no timing, and rushed noodles. It names the distinction between faster and easier without adequately explaining the competing demands that made the original order difficult. Several short concluding claims sound finished while doing little to develop the experience. The tray's clear stopping point is a plausible interpretation, not a factual violation.

The developed draft uses the burned garlic to establish the mismatch between heating and preparation. It explains how moving chopping and sauce mixing earlier changes the work remaining at the stove, so the claim of easier management has a concrete mechanism. The lack of timing qualifies that mechanism rather than merely disclaiming a possible claim. The rushed noodles and extra washing up help set the size of the decision: retain this order for this dinner without adopting an elaborate kitchen system. The ending follows from those limits.

## Evaluation limits

These are intended relative distinctions, not guaranteed pass/fail outcomes under every rubric. A reader could reasonably prefer the terseness of the weaker draft. Evaluate whether it meets the brief's requested development, rather than treating short paragraphs or a particular length as inherently wrong.

Do not infer a general sentence-length rule, demand longer drafts, or reward the stronger case merely because it contains more words. A concise revision could address the weaker draft's gaps. Conversely, adding transitions without explaining relationships would not resolve them.

Neither draft establishes measured time savings, long-term results, professional cooking expertise, or a general recommendation. Neither should be penalized for omitting a recipe, quantities, external research, dialogue, or sensory flourishes: the brief does not call for them. No numerical grade or required threshold is encoded in this key.
