# Before I turn on the stove

Tofu, cabbage, and noodles are a regular after-work dinner for me. I usually heat the pan while I get everything ready. It feels like a reasonable way to get dinner moving.

Then I burned the garlic while cutting the cabbage. The pan was ready before I was.

For the next three dinners, I changed the order. I drained and cut the tofu, sliced the cabbage, and mixed the sauce before turning on the stove.

I put the prepared ingredients on a tray beside the pan. Everything I needed was together, and the tray gave the preparation a clear stopping point.

There were more bowls to wash. That was the tradeoff, and it belongs in any account of how well this worked.

Once I started cooking, I could stay at the stove. I wasn't leaving the pan to finish chopping or mix the sauce. That felt easier to manage.

I didn't time any of these dinners. I can't say that preparing everything first was faster. Faster and easier are different things.

The noodles still felt rushed at the end. Preparation helped, but it didn't fix every part of making dinner.

I'll keep getting the tofu, cabbage, and sauce ready before heating the pan. I don't need a separate bowl for every ingredient. I just need to be ready for the next step.
