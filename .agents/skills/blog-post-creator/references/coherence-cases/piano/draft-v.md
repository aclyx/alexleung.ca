After twelve years away from the piano, I am learning one waltz to play with my sister at our grandfather's eightieth birthday. She plays violin, and we rehearse together on Sundays.

I practice alone for twenty minutes on three evenings each week, with a metronome and pencil marks beside the difficult bars. Alone, I can stop whenever I miss something. In the duet, my sister keeps playing, and I have to find my way back in. Getting through a passage by myself does not necessarily mean I can stay with her through it.

The repetition is tedious. I think it is worth doing because it lets us play together on Sundays, and I would want those sessions even without the birthday. I am not trying to recover my old level or learn a stack of new pieces. Returning to piano has given me a regular reason to spend time with my sister. The solitary work is the price I am willing to pay for that.
