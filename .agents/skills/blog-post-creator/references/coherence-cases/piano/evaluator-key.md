# Evaluator key — keep separate from the test inputs

## Intended distinction

Draft U needs a focused repair to its final paragraph. It changes the author's stated judgment: the source describes tedious solo work as a cost worth paying for shared time, while U says the repetition is becoming pleasurable and the practice its own reward. The source supplies neither change. Restore the relationship between the disliked preparation and the valued Sunday sessions, including their value beyond the birthday performance.

Draft V expresses the source relationships without adding facts. Both drafts identify the concrete performance goal at the start, and both include plausible practice details. This keeps the comparison from resting on an obviously incomprehensible opening or on the mere presence of practical details.

## What the case exercises

- Reader orientation: the instrument, collaborator, piece, occasion, and immediate goal are identifiable without background knowledge.
- Personal meaning: the author values a recurring reason to spend time with the sister. The metronome, pencil marks, and practice schedule support that idea but cannot replace it.
- Explanatory value: the contrast between stopping alone and rejoining a moving duet explains why solo preparation serves shared playing. It is more than a list of actions or a generic claim that practice matters.
- Fidelity to judgment: the author accepts a tedious cost for a specific benefit. An editor should not make the account more reassuring by claiming the cost has become enjoyable.

## Sentence to protect in V

“Getting through a passage by myself does not necessarily mean I can stay with her through it.”

This sentence draws out the consequence of the two practice conditions already supplied in the brief. It explains the distinction between solo completion and coordination. Although it follows two concrete sentences about the same situation, it is not merely a restatement or reassurance and should not be mechanically removed as filler.

## Scope of the repair

Do not infer that any mention of a routine is undesirable or that the author must reject technique. The concrete details are supported and useful. The repair is to the draft's account of what the work is for and how the author values its cost, not to its vocabulary or factual practice schedule.
