After twelve years away from the piano, I am learning a waltz to play with my sister at our grandfather's eightieth birthday. She plays violin. We rehearse on Sundays, and I practice alone for twenty minutes on three evenings during the week.

The arrangement is straightforward: a metronome for keeping time, pencil marks for the difficult bars, and enough repetition to get through them. Alone, I can stop and begin again. With my sister still playing, I have to find my way back in. That gives the practice a different shape.

I am not trying to recover all my old ability or build a larger repertoire. For now, the small routine is enough. The solo repetition can be tedious, but it is becoming part of the pleasure of returning. The birthday gives us a destination; the practice is becoming its own reward.
