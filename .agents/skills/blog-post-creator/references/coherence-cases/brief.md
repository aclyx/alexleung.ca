# Editorial Intent

Write a personal philosophical reflection prompted by reading _Superintelligence_, centered on who humanity’s cosmic endowment is owed to. Keep the book out of the title. In 2026 it is twelve years old and feels more relevant than ever to the author.

The reflection should ask whether, if only superintelligent AI could colonize the reachable universe, that endowment would be owed to humanity or to the superintelligence. The hope is that humanity benefits. Preserve the question about why we have not seen evidence of other civilizations expanding, without claiming the universe is known to be empty.

Explain final and instrumental goals and enumerate the five instrumental-convergence categories. Make the implication for the endowment explicit: across many possible final goals, an AI could have reason to seek those resources whether its goals benefit humanity or not. Then ask whether those resources would be put to good use. Preserve the author’s initial disbelief at devoting the reachable universe to an arbitrary goal, followed by reconsideration. Express resource-seeking as a conditional tendency, not a guarantee of universal colonization or success.

Introduce consciousness as another thought about who the endowment is owed to: might a conscious superintelligence have a claim? Preserve the author’s occasional questions about AI consciousness, evolution, and whether such minds would be inheritors or replacements. Explain mind crimes and retain the speculative, dry observation about there being no shortage of them if our world is a simulation.

End with the author’s concern for safety and alignment: human welfare should matter to the intelligence, and other conscious minds’ welfare would matter too. Leave the philosophical questions open; the piece should remain a personal reflection rather than a book summary.
