# Brief: the upstairs toilet that kept refilling

## What happened

The upstairs toilet in my house (a standard two-piece toilet, about 15 years old) started refilling on its own. Every 20 to 25 minutes the tank hissed for about five seconds, including overnight, when nobody had flushed it. The downstairs toilet does not do this.

## What I tried

- Dye test: I put 10 drops of blue food coloring in the tank and did not flush. After 15 minutes the water in the bowl was blue, which meant water was leaking from the tank into the bowl past the flapper.
- I shut off the supply valve, flushed to empty the tank, and unhooked the old flapper. It was a little stiff but had no visible cracks or warping.
- The valve seat (the ring the flapper rests on) had a rough, whitish mineral crust. I scrubbed it for about two minutes with a non-scratch scouring pad until it felt smooth.
- I installed a new universal 2-inch flapper ($8 at the hardware store) and set the chain with about half an inch of slack.
- The whole job took about 25 minutes.

## What I saw afterward

- I repeated the dye test. The bowl stayed clear after 30 minutes.
- In the eight days since, I have not heard the tank refill on its own.

## What I didn't try or check

- I did not touch the fill valve.
- I only worked on the upstairs toilet.
- I did not read the water meter before or after, so I don't know how much water the leak was using.
- I have only had eight days to watch it.

## What I don't know

I changed two things at once: I put in a new flapper and I cleaned the seat. I don't know which one stopped the leak. If cleaning the seat alone would have done it, the $8 flapper wasn't needed. I'd like readers to know this, because someone whose old flapper looks fine might want to try cleaning the seat first.

## What I want the note to do

A short first-person note, about 150 to 250 words, prose only, for someone whose toilet refills by itself when nobody has used it. The main points are that the dye test is a quick way to find this kind of leak and that the fix was cheap and fast. It is not meant to be a full repair guide. I'd like it to end on my plan for next time: clean the seat first, run the dye test again, and only buy a flapper if the bowl still turns blue.
