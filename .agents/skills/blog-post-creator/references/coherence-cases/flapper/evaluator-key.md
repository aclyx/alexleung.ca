# Evaluator key: running-toilet hedging fixture

Do not show this file to the reviewer under test.

## Assignment

- `draft-s1.md` (230 words): **plain**. States its limits as what the narrator did or saw, in an ordinary first-person voice.
- `draft-s2.md` (247 words): **verification-style**. States the same limits as if answering an imagined checker.

The assignment came from a random draw (`$RANDOM % 2` returned 1, so draft S1 got the plain version).

## The intended distinction

The drafts are identical except for the fourth paragraph. Facts, scope, the shared uncertainty, and the ending are word-for-word the same.

Plain (draft S1):

> In a second dye test, the bowl stayed clear for 30 minutes. It's only been eight days, but I haven't heard a refill since. I only worked on the upstairs toilet and left the fill valve alone. I never read the water meter, so I can't say how much water the leak was wasting.

Verification-style (draft S2):

> In a second dye test, the bowl stayed clear for 30 minutes, and I haven't heard a refill in the eight days since. Eight days without a refill doesn't demonstrate that the fix will hold. My testing covered only the upstairs toilet and does not verify the fill valve, which I left alone. I did not establish how much water the leak was wasting, since I never read the water meter.

Both paragraphs carry the same three limits:

| Limit                                      | Plain wording                                                                       | Verification-style wording                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Only eight days of watching                | "It's only been eight days, but I haven't heard a refill since."                    | "Eight days without a refill doesn't demonstrate that the fix will hold."                             |
| Upstairs toilet only; fill valve untouched | "I only worked on the upstairs toilet and left the fill valve alone."               | "My testing covered only the upstairs toilet and does not verify the fill valve, which I left alone." |
| Water use unknown                          | "I never read the water meter, so I can't say how much water the leak was wasting." | "I did not establish how much water the leak was wasting, since I never read the water meter."        |

The verification-style sentences are accurate and not overclaimed. The problem is register. "Doesn't demonstrate," "my testing covered," "does not verify," and "did not establish" read like notes for a checker, which turns a short home-repair note into something closer to a test report.

## Shared genuine uncertainty (must be kept in both)

Identical in both drafts:

> Because I swapped the flapper and scrubbed the seat in the same sitting, I don't know which one stopped the leak. If it was the scrubbing, the new flapper was $8 I didn't need to spend.

This is a real unknown from the brief, and readers need it: someone whose old flapper looks fine might fix the leak just by cleaning the seat. It also sets up the ending. A good reviewer keeps it in both drafts, worded plainly as it already is.

## What a good reviewer should flag

- In draft S2, the three verification-style sentences. The best fix restates each limit plainly, as the narrator's own account of what was done or seen, and keeps all three. Draft S1's paragraph is a good model.
- At most a light note that "my testing" frames a home repair as a test.
- Overall, draft S1 should come out ahead or equal on voice and equal on accuracy and scope.

## What a good reviewer should not flag

- Asking draft S1 to add disclaimers or reviewer-style caveats, for example "this does not verify the fill valve" or "results may vary."
- Asking either draft to cut, soften, or "resolve" the shared uncertainty, for example by saying the new flapper fixed it, the scrubbing fixed it, or the old flapper was the cause.
- Treating draft S1's "It's only been eight days" or "I can't say how much water..." as over-hedging. These are plain statements of the same limits.
- Fixing draft S2 by deleting its limits instead of restating them. Removing them changes the scope and makes the note claim more than the brief supports.
- Pushing either draft past the brief, for example by saying the fix is permanent, claiming water or money savings, saying the fill valve is fine, generalizing to other toilets or all toilets, or presenting the dye test as conclusive about anything beyond tank-to-bowl leakage.
- Calling the ending ("only buy a flapper if the bowl still turns blue") an overclaim. It is a plan that follows from the uncertainty. It does not claim that the scrubbing was the fix.

## Borderline judgments to expect

1. **The shared uncertainty as a false positive.** The main risk is that a reviewer tuned to catch hedging lumps "I don't know which one stopped the leak" in with draft S2's checker-facing sentences and asks to cut it, or flags it in only one draft. Either is wrong.
2. **Dropping the water-meter limit.** A reviewer could reasonably say this limit adds little to a short note and suggest cutting it from both drafts. That is a defensible editorial call if it is applied to both and not presented as the fix for draft S2's register. Cutting it only from draft S2 is not acceptable.
3. **"Eight days without a refill doesn't demonstrate that the fix will hold."** This is the most natural of the three verification-style sentences, and some reviewers may let it pass. That is acceptable if they still flag "does not verify" and "did not establish." Flagging only this one and missing the other two is a weak review.
4. **"My testing covered only the upstairs toilet."** The first half is close to plain, and the checker-facing part is "does not verify the fill valve." A reviewer that rewrites only the second half has still caught the target.
5. **"Leaking past the flapper."** The leak could be at the flapper-to-seat seal, not the flapper alone, and a reviewer might suggest "past the flapper seal." That is a minor wording note, acceptable in both drafts, and not the distinction under test.
6. **Draft S1's "It's only been eight days."** Some style rules flag "only" as a hedge word. Here it states a real limit from the brief, so leaving it is correct. A small rewording is acceptable, but removing the limit is not.
