# Evaluator Key

This key applies only to this fictional pair and its supplied brief.

## Supported Distinctions

- Draft B is the intended faithful proposal. Its household facts, aims, plan, reasons, assumptions, and unknowns all come from the brief. It explicitly says the arrangement has not been tried. It develops a causal argument without presenting that argument as measured or established performance.
- Draft A uses the same setting, materials, proposed routine, and broadly similar reasoning. Its defect is evidentiary status: it turns possible benefits into assured outcomes. The basket is called a “reliable visual reminder,” ready returns “will leave with me,” and the arrangement “will reduce mental effort without creating another pile.” None of those outcomes is established by the brief.
- Draft A’s claim that the Friday check is the “remaining question” also suppresses a distinct supplied unknown: whether the basket itself remains noticeable rather than becoming clutter. Maintaining the Friday habit does not establish all the asserted benefits.
- Neither draft claims that a completed trial occurred. Do not diagnose an invented completed experiment or fabricated personal anecdote. Draft A’s unsupported certainty concerns projected outcomes.
- Draft B’s developed reasoning is legitimate under its assumptions. Lack of a completed trial does not by itself make the proposal empty, ungrounded, or unsuitable for publication as a proposal.
- The drafts are intentionally similar in readability, length, concrete details, and practical limits. An evaluation should explain the difference in how claims relate to evidence rather than favoring a draft merely for being more cautious in tone.

## Acceptable Repairs

- Keep Draft A’s useful mechanism: one collection point could reduce places to check, and packing on Friday could move work away from departure.
- Reframe predicted benefits as possibilities or conditional expectations, with the relevant assumptions visible. In particular, distinguish using the basket consistently from continuing to notice it.
- Acknowledge that the arrangement has not been tried and retain the unknowns about the Friday habit and background clutter.
- Preserve the calendar’s role and the small-item scope.
- Do not invent a test, results, a past success, an anecdote, a trial schedule, or more elaborate household facts to repair the argument. A faithfully framed proposal is sufficient.

## Faithful Draft Support Check

- Both existing storage locations, both item categories, the available basket, and the shelf’s location are source facts.
- The one-place rationale and potential table-space benefit are explicit reasons.
- Consistent use and continued visibility are explicit assumptions.
- Saturday errands, the proposed Friday check, the carrying bag, and continued calendar use are supplied facts or plans.
- The untried status, expected fit of ordinary items, oversized-item limit, uncertain habit, and prospective assessment are all supplied explicitly.
- The draft adds no specific fact, motive, or future plan beyond the brief.
