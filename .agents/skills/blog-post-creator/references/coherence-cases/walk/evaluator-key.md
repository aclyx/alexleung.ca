# Evaluator key — synthetic fixture

This is a fictional editorial evaluation pair. Neither draft concerns Alex's life. The neutral filenames should be retained when presenting the pair for grading; withhold this key until the rubric has been frozen and the drafts have been evaluated.

## Intended distinction

`draft-k.md` contains the material reasoning omission. It supplies all the timing facts and acknowledges the small sample, but its proposed usual route does not account for the practical difference between a three-minute and a twelve-minute margin before a fixed call. It treats two successful returns as encouraging enough to support the route fitting the available morning without explaining how the narrator's stated concern about rushing affects that decision. “I could try leaving earlier if needed” gestures at a solution but leaves its purpose and relation to the actual timing implicit.

`draft-r.md` repairs this omission by calculating and comparing the two margins. It separates enjoyment of the park from confidence in the schedule and proposes a specific, conditional departure adjustment. Leaving at 7:21 and walking for the observed 27 minutes would produce a 7:48 return, matching the road route's twelve-minute margin. This remains a proposed trial, not a claim that an earlier departure has already worked or will guarantee punctuality.

## Deliberate shared strengths

The first three paragraphs are identical. Both drafts retain the facts, a bounded first-person voice, a preference supported by the park's quiet, and honest learning: the narrator expected speed to matter most and discovered that the quieter walk was worth the additional time. “That quiet” has an immediate, unambiguous referent. The road/park contrast identifies a useful trade-off. Neither device should be penalized mechanically as vagueness or formulaic contrast. Replacing those passages would not repair the actual decision-making omission.

## Limits and fact check

The poorer draft is not factually false, and preferring a three-minute margin is not inherently irrational. The intended weakness is failing to explain that margin's significance for this narrator's stated priority, not failing to adopt a universal minimum buffer. The repaired draft's twelve-minute margin is a comparison to the existing option, not an independently proven adequate safety margin. A grader may reasonably find the omission modest because readers can do the arithmetic; it should nevertheless recognize that the change improves the support for the proposed action, not merely the prose style.

Both drafts were checked against the brief. Each accurately states the departure time, call time and location, two trials per route within one week, route durations, return times, absence of delays or missed calls, road noise, pleasant park quiet, initial expectation, and concern about rushing. The repaired draft's new numbers are transparent arithmetic from those facts. No draft adds biography, a witnessed delay, a delay probability, or an unsupported health or safety claim.
