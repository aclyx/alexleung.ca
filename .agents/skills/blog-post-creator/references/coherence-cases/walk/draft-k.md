I expected the shorter route to be the easy choice for a morning walk. I leave at 7:30 and need to be home for an eight o'clock call. Getting back reliably matters to me, and I dislike feeling rushed before it.

Over one week, I tried each route twice. The road route follows a busy street and took 18 minutes both times, putting me home at 7:48. The park route took 27 minutes both times, putting me home at 7:57. There were no delays, and I did not miss a call.

The park was quieter. That quiet made the extra walking time feel worthwhile, which surprised me. I had assumed that finishing sooner would matter most, but the walk itself turned out to matter too. The road route gives me a shorter outing; the park gives me a more pleasant one.

I am leaning toward making the park my usual route. Getting home before eight on both attempts is encouraging, and I could try leaving earlier if needed. Four walks are not enough to tell me how dependable either route will be. For now, though, the park seems to offer the quiet I enjoyed while still fitting into the morning I have available.
