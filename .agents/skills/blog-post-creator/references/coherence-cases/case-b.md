# Humanity’s Cosmic Endowment

I recently finished Nick Bostrom’s [_Superintelligence_](https://nickbostrom.com/). It is twelve years old as of 2026, but it feels more relevant than ever.

Humanity’s cosmic endowment is the matter and energy a future civilization could reach. Those resources could support [lives far beyond Earth](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/astronomical-waste/). If only a superintelligent AI could colonize the reachable universe, would that cosmic endowment be owed to humanity or to the superintelligence?

That made me think about other civilizations that might have developed a superintelligence capable of expanding beyond their home world. [Why haven’t we found evidence of that expansion?](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/search-for-life/)

## What would the endowment be used for?

The same resources that could support a human future could serve a goal that has no place for us. A final goal is an end in itself; instrumental goals help achieve it.

[Instrumental convergence](https://nickbostrom.com/superintelligentwill.pdf) is the idea that systems with very different final goals can have reasons to pursue the same intermediate goals. Bostrom identifies five categories: self-preservation, preserving one’s goals, improving one’s reasoning, improving one’s tools, and acquiring resources.

At first, I found it hard to accept that a superintelligence might devote the entire reachable universe to an arbitrary final goal. It seemed absurd, but on reflection it makes sense: a superintelligence could find ways to pursue its final goal that are far beyond us. If more resources helped, it could put all the matter and energy it could reach toward that goal.

## Conscious minds

But if the intelligence that reaches those resources were conscious, would we owe it a place in that future too? I sometimes wonder what would make an AI conscious, and whether such minds could be a continuation of our evolution, eventually becoming a species of their own. Would I see them as inheritors of a human future, or as something that had replaced us?

A [mind crime](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/aipolicy.pdf) could involve creating conscious beings within a simulation and subjecting them to suffering. The harm would occur inside the computation, even if its outputs harmed no one outside it. If we created such minds, running, copying, or shutting down a simulation could become decisions about their lives.

Reading about mind crimes, I also found myself wondering whether we could be inside a simulation. If so, there seems to have been no shortage of mind crimes in this simulation.

## A future for humanity

I hope humanity gets to inherit that future, but I can’t take that for granted just because we might create the intelligence that reaches it. Safety and alignment are top of mind for me now. I want humanity’s welfare to be part of what that intelligence ultimately values. If other conscious minds share that future, their welfare would matter too.
