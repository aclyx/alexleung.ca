# Darn your sweaters before you replace them

When a wool sweater gets a hole at the elbow, the usual move is to start shopping for a new one. There’s another option worth trying first: you could darn it.

A darning needle and a small skein of matching wool might cost about six dollars. One tutorial would probably be enough to get started, and a thumbnail-sized hole could take a single evening. The patch might not match perfectly. It might show, and it might feel a little stiff where the rows overlap. You would still have the sweater.

The bigger gain could be in how you treat it afterward. Someone who has spent an evening on a repair would likely check the other elbow sooner. They might start folding the sweater instead of leaving it over a chair. Wear that used to go unnoticed until it became a hole could start to show up earlier, because you would know what fixing it costs.

In that sense, an evening of darning might buy you more attention than it buys you sweater. That could be the better half of the deal.

There’s one risk worth knowing about. A darn sewn too tight can pull out at its edges, and a beginner might not be able to tell whether the tension is right. If a first darn holds through a winter of regular wear, it would be worth doing the other elbow before it opens. Either way, it’s an easy thing to try.
