# Darning a sweater instead of replacing it

## What happened

- In October I found a thumbnail-sized hole at the left elbow of a grey merino sweater I've worn for about six years.
- I usually replace a sweater once it has a hole. This time I watched one tutorial, bought a darning needle and a small skein of grey wool for $6, and darned it in one evening.
- It took about an hour and a half. The yarn is a shade darker, so the patch shows, and it's a little stiff where the rows overlap.
- I've worn the sweater four times since. The darn hasn't come apart.

## What I took from it

- I didn't expect the repair to change how I treat the sweater, but it did. The next morning I checked the other elbow and found it thinning. I now fold the sweater instead of leaving it over a chair.
- Before, I only noticed wear once it was a hole. Now I look for it earlier, because I know what fixing it costs. The repair changed my attention more than my wardrobe, and I think the attention was the better part of the trade.

## What I don't know

- Whether the darn survives a winter of regular wear. The tutorial said a darn sewn too tight pulls out at its edges, and I can't tell whether mine is. I won't know until spring.
- If it holds, I plan to darn the other elbow before it opens.
