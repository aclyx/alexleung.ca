# Darning my grey sweater

In October I found a thumbnail-sized hole in the left elbow of a grey merino sweater I’ve worn for about six years. Until now, a hole meant I started shopping for a replacement.

This time I watched one tutorial, bought a darning needle and a small skein of grey wool for six dollars, and spent an evening on it. It took about an hour and a half. The yarn turned out a shade darker than the sweater, so the patch shows, and it’s a little stiff where the rows overlap. I’ve worn the sweater four times since and the darn hasn’t come apart.

What I didn’t expect was how it changed the way I treat the sweater. The next morning I checked the other elbow and found it thinning. I’ve started folding the sweater instead of leaving it over a chair. Before the repair, wear was something I noticed only once it became a hole. Now I look for it earlier, because I know what fixing it costs.

The hour and a half bought me more attention than it bought me sweater. That seems like the better half of the deal.

What I don’t know yet is whether the darn will last. The tutorial warned that a darn sewn too tight pulls out at its edges, and I can’t tell whether mine is too tight. If it holds through a winter of regular wear, I’ll darn the other elbow before it opens. I won’t know until spring.
