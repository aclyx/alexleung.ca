18:T99a,<p>I recently finished Nick Bostrom’s <a href="https://nickbostrom.com/"><em>Superintelligence</em></a>. I wonder whether conscious AI could inherit humanity’s future, or whether I would see it as having replaced us.</p>
<p>“Cosmic endowment” is a way of thinking about the matter and energy a future civilization could reach. Those resources could support <a href="https://nickbostrom.com/papers/astronomical-waste/">lives far beyond Earth</a>. If only a superintelligent AI could colonize the reachable universe, would that endowment be owed to humanity or to the superintelligence?</p>
<h2>What would the endowment be used for?</h2>
<p>At first, the idea of devoting the reachable universe to an arbitrary goal seemed absurd to me. But more matter and energy could give a superintelligence more computing power to pursue that goal.</p>
<p>This is one example of <a href="https://nickbostrom.com/superintelligentwill.pdf">instrumental convergence</a>: systems pursuing very different ends can have reasons to seek the same means. An AI could have a reason to acquire resources throughout space without caring about the lives those resources might otherwise support. Its expansion would not, by itself, secure a future for humanity.</p>
<h2>Conscious minds</h2>
<p>If such an AI were conscious, though, would it have a claim of its own? I sometimes wonder what would make an AI conscious, and whether such minds could be a continuation of our evolution, eventually becoming a species of their own.</p>
<p>That question extends to conscious minds we might create inside simulations. If their lives mattered, we would have responsibilities toward them. Subjecting them to suffering would be an example of a <a href="https://nickbostrom.com/papers/aipolicy.pdf">mind crime</a>: harm within a computation, even if its outputs harmed no one outside it. Running, copying, or shutting down such a simulation could become decisions about those lives.</p>
<h2>A future for humanity</h2>
<p>I hope humanity inherits the cosmic endowment and uses it to support lives beyond Earth. A superintelligence could instead use those resources to pursue a goal that has no place for us. The possibility of losing so much of humanity’s future is why safety and alignment are top of mind for me now. I want humanity’s welfare to be part of what that intelligence ultimately values.
